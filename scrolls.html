      Rules Recap

      General Rules

      Bonuses and Maluses

      • All modifiers to rolls are capped at +1 or −1.
      • If the total of all bonuses would give +2 or more, treat it as +1.
      • If the total of all maluses would give −2 or more, treat it as −1.

      Objective Control

      • To control an objective, you must have the highest degree of control over it at the end of a turn.
      • Once an objective is secured, it remains under your control until an enemy unit takes control from you.

      Underdog Bonus

      • The player controlling fewer objectives is the “underdog”.
      • If you are the underdog at the start of a turn, you may use one free card that turn.

      Victory Points

      Scoring from Objectives

      Victory points (VP) are awarded as follows:
      • 1 VP for controlling one objective.
      • 1 VP for controlling two objectives.
      • 1 VP for controlling more objectives than your opponent.
      • 1 VP for each more valuable objective you control.

      Missions

      • Missions are specific to each map and depend on the map’s layout and conditions.

      Tactics

      • At the start of each round, both you and your opponent may declare a tactic.
      • A tactic is a self‑written strategy goal (for example: “Kill the Distant: one enemy ranged unit with at least 5 starting tokens must be killed”).
      • Tactics must be accepted by your opponent to be valid.
      • A successfully completed tactic grants 2 VP.
      • If a tactic is not completed by the end of the round, it instead grants 1 VP to your opponent.
      Example Tactics
      • Take Their Land – Secure a foothold within enemy territory:
        • Pick a terrain feature that is wholly or partially within enemy territory and fully outside friendly territory.
        • You complete this tactic if you control that terrain feature at the end of your turn.

      How the Opponent Scores

      Your opponent gains VP from:
      • Killing one of your hero units.
      • Objectives (as described above).
      • Specific declared missions.

      Hero Units

      • During the deployment phase, you must assign each of your characters to a different unit.
      • Each assigned unit gains a label:
        • Demir, Darius: Priest.
        • Hor, Minerva: Wizard.
        • Arren: Warrior.
      • If a hero unit is killed by the enemy:
        • The enemy gains 1 VP.
        • The assigned character loses half of their hit points.
        • The assigned character gains 2 exhaustion points until the next long rest.

      Formations

      • A unit is considered “in formation” only if all of its tokens are arranged in that formation.
      • You cannot create a formation with a unit that has fewer than 3 tokens.
      • Formations may only be created if there is enough space on the terrain to place them as intended.

      Breaking a Formation

      • After a charge, check each unit in your formation that is in combat against your opponent, using a Risk‑style roll‑off.
      • For each unit in combat, roll a d6.
      • If you win more than half of the rolls, you may move 2 units to break the formation.
      • In ties, the result goes to the defender.

      Line Formation

      • Declare a front of the line.
      • Units gain +1 to saves against melee attacks that come from enemy tokens on the front of the line.
      • Units suffer −1 to saves against melee attacks that come from enemy tokens behind the line.
      • Saves against attacks from the sides are unaffected.

      Spread Formation

      • Grants +1 to saves against ranged attacks.
      • Grants -1 to saves against melee attacks.
      • Charging units gain +1 additional damage during the turn they charge.

      Spear Formation

      • You may re‑roll any failed formation break roll.
      • Units suffer −1 to saves against both ranged and melee attacks while in this formation.

      Column Formation

      • Requires at least 1 unit.
      • Grants +2 movement to the unit(s) in the formation.
      • Units suffer −1 to saves against melee attacks.

      Formations and Movement

      • Each token is limited by its base movement and can never move farther than that value.
      • For simplicity, when moving a formation, you may move the center of the formation and freely rotate it around the ending position.
      • Entering a formation always costs 1 point of movement.
      • During the fight phase, you may perform a “shuffle in”.
      • In a shuffle in, each token may move up to 3 inches to enter melee with a target unit; doing so breaks the formation.
      • Units that are already in melee cannot shuffle in.
      • If a shuffling unit would end its move already in melee, it stops short instead.

      Terrain Rules

      Terrain features may have one or more of the following attributes:

      Cover

      • Subtract 1 from wound rolls for attacks that target a unit that is behind or wholly on terrain with the “Cover” attribute.
      • This modifier does not apply if the unit charged this turn or has the Fly keyword.

      Impassable

      • Models cannot move across, be set up on, or end any move on any part of an Impassable terrain feature.

      Obscuring

      • Enemy units that are not within combat range cannot target a unit with shooting attacks if that unit is behind or wholly on an Obscuring terrain feature.
      • This restriction does not apply if the targeted unit has the Fly keyword.

      Unstable

      • Models can move across Unstable terrain.
      • Models cannot be set up on, or end any type of move on, any part of Unstable terrain that is more than 1 inch tall.

      Height Rules

      • Each map has three height layers: −2, −1, 0, +1, and +2.
      • Attacks from a unit at one height layer to a unit at another height layer receive a bonus equal to the difference in layers.
      • For example:
        • A shooting attack from layer +1 to layer −1 has a +2 bonus to the roll.
        • A shooting attack from layer −2 to layer 0 has a −2 penalty to the roll.

      Height in Melee and Charges

      • The same height‑difference bonus or penalty applies to melee attacks when the units are at different layers.
      • For example, if one unit is at layer 0 and the other at layer 1, melee rolls are affected by the +1/−1 difference.
      • When a unit charges from one layer to another (for example, charging from layer 0 to a unit at layer 1), the charge and attack rolls are affected by the height difference, even if the charge fully moves the charging unit into the new layer.

      Weather

      Different weather conditions affect fighting conditions. The possible conditions and their effects are listed below.

      • Rain: Units cannot catch fire and are considered wet.
      • Snow: Wet units may move or charge only half their normal movement and charge values.
      • Heavy Snow: All effects of Snow apply, and all units have their movement reduced by 2.
      • Heavy Fog: All enemy units more than 6" away from an allied unit are treated as if behind Obscuring terrain.
      • Extreme Heat: Units on fire receive an additional D3 mortal wounds at the end of their turn. Units cannot be wet.
      • Sandstorm: All effects of Heavy Fog apply, and all units have their movement reduced by 1".

      Wind

      Wind is defined by both direction and intensity. It may also be static or inconsistent — if static, the direction remains constant each round; if inconsistent, roll a D6 at the start of each new round to determine the wind direction.

      • Moderate: Archery (±1), Gunpowder (±0)
      • Strong: Archery (±2), Gunpowder (±1)
      • Hurricane: Archery (±2), Gunpowder (±2). Units with the Fly keyword are treated as if they do not have it.

      Wind directions are defined by cardinal direction only. When shooting with the wind (target is downwind), add the modifier to your hit roll. When shooting against the wind (target is upwind), subtract the modifier from your hit roll.
